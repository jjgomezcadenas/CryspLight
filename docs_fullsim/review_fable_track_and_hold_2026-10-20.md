# Referee report: "Why track and hold" (bench/rationale_for_DAQ_PR.tex, section 2)

Date: 2026-10-20. Scope: the claim that the Citiroc track-and-hold (T&H) mode is the better way to calibrate the SiPM channels (pedestal, hold-delay scan, SPE gain) compared with the peak detector (PEAK_STRETCH), and the DAQ pull requests #73–#75 that implement it. Sources read: the rationale, the bench notes (bench/1_citiroc_noise_v2.tex, bench/v1/2_citiroc_hold_track.tex), the toy (bench/hold_scan_figs.py), the Citiroc 1A datasheet text (papers/citiroc1a-datasheet.txt), FERSlib/Janus 5.0.1, pet-caen-daq at 75086b2 plus the three PR diffs, the design note (docs_fullsim/fullsim_design.tex) and the simulation (src/frontend/citiroc.jl, fullsim_analysis/scripts/calibration.jl). Sizes of effects below come from a short calculation with the toy's own pulse and filter model (SiPM pulse exp(−t/100) − exp(−t/50) ns, CR-RC² with θ = t_p/2, fast shaper 15 ns, threshold 0.5 pe, dark rate 4.4 MHz); they describe the model, not yet the chip.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The section is right that T&H gives a direct single-sample noise and maps the shaped pulse, and the plan to calibrate in both modes is sound. But "the better way to calibrate" is not justified as written. Claim (a) misdescribes the peak detector (the maximum of two non-overlapping pulses is the larger one, not their sum; pile-up onto the stored value is of the same order in both modes). Claim (c) holds only for the channel that caused the common trigger, and the selection that PR #75 adds (the hit-register flag) does not identify that channel; the per-channel ToA, which does, is decoded in the scan loop and thrown away. In T&H the time walk of the fast discriminator biases the ladder slope low by about 4% at 25 ns shaping and 1% at 87.5 ns, an effect the peak detector does not have. For the SPE gain of all 64 channels at once, the peak detector with a periodic trigger is the simpler and less biased measurement; T&H is the right tool for the pulse map and the noise. The text should be qualified accordingly, and PR #75 should keep the timing information.

## 1. Common hold, selection of events, time walk (question 1)

What the hardware does. The A5202 holds all channels with one signal: the datasheet states "all the 32 channels are hold at the same time" (papers/citiroc1a-datasheet.txt:312) and FERSlib arms the peak detector from the external global trigger, not from each channel's own discriminator (FERS_configure_5202.c:245, bit 309 = 1; datasheet:437–438, 715–717). The hold comes HoldDelay after the FPGA's bunch trigger (FERS_Registers_520X.h:84 "Time from gtrg to peak hold"; FERS_configure_5202.c:254 writes HoldDelay/8 ns). So in T&H only channels whose own crossing coincides with the trigger (within a few ns) are sampled at their peak; every other channel is sampled at a random phase of its own dark-pulse train.

Does the "discriminator flag" of PR #75 select those channels? No. The flag is bit 15 of the energy word (pet-caen-daq backend/internal/dt5202/event.go:248–251), which FERSlib decodes as qdmask (FERS_readout.c:846, 853): the latched hit register of the Citiroc "charge discriminator" (datasheet:495–506; FERSlib bit 258, EnableQdiscrLatch, FERS_configure_5202.c:231). It records that the channel's fast-shaper signal crossed QD_CoarseThreshold at some time while the latch gate (ValEvt, propagated from the FPGA gate, FERS_configure_5202.c:248) was open, with no timing. Neither the DAQ nor the FERSlib configuration states the extent of that gate relative to the trigger. At 4.4 MHz per channel an unrelated dark pulse sets the flag with probability 1 − exp(−4.4 MHz × W): 36% for a 100 ns gate, 73% for 300 ns. The flagged spectrum is therefore a mixture of the triggering channel (sampled at the peak) and channels sampled at random phase; the unflagged spectrum is a random-phase spectrum with pulses below threshold or outside the gate. PR #75's description ("the ones holding their pulse") and the rationale's line 48 ("the SPE spectrum is taken at the maximum of that pulse") assume a selection that the flag does not make. The earlier plan (bench/v1/2_citiroc_hold_track.tex:253–268) had this right: SPECT_TIMING, trigger T-OR with OR64, and separation by the per-channel ToA. The DAQ's scan already decodes the timing qualifier (backend/internal/holddelay/scan.go:168 calls DecodeSpectroscopy, which fills Timings, event.go:60–72) but PR #75 keeps only energies by flag. Selecting the channel with the earliest ToA (0.5 ns LSB) in each event, or all channels within ±3 ns of it, is the clean selection; the PR lists it as a "possible follow-up", it should be the main path.

Near-coincident triggers from other channels. With 64 channels at 4.4 MHz the board sees a crossing every 3.6 ns on average. The event's hold is set by the first crossing after the dead time; a channel that crosses Δ ns later is sampled Δ ns before its peak. The loss is quadratic near the flat top: at 25 ns shaping a 4 ns offset costs 1.2%, 8 ns 1.4% on average (0.4% at 87.5 ns), so a ToA window of a few ns around the trigger costs nothing measurable and raises the statistics (about 1 + 0.28 × window[ns] channels per event are "at peak").

Time walk. In the toy model the 0.5 pe threshold is crossed at 14, 9 and 8 ns for 1, 2 and 3 pe pulses: 2 and 3 pe pulses trigger 5–6 ns earlier and are sampled 5–6 ns after their peak. With the hold delay chosen to maximise the 1 pe peak, the 2 and 3 pe positions fall to 0.981 and 0.974 of their true value at 25 ns shaping (0.995, 0.993 at 87.5 ns; 0.966, 0.952 at 12.5 ns). The straight line through the 1-2-3 ladder then returns a gain 3.9% low at 25 ns, 1% at 87.5 ns, 7% at 12.5 ns. This is the same order as the 3.2% shift the design note reports for its common-hold test (fullsim_design.tex:1216). The simulation reproduces the walk (it holds at the crossing of the real fast-shaped pulse, citiroc.jl:256–268), so it is not a model-vs-bench discrepancy, but it is a mode-specific systematic that must be corrected or absorbed by using the 1 pe peak alone, or by a longer shaping.

Clock quantisation. The gtrg and the hold are synchronous to the 8 ns clock while the crossing is not: the hold phase is uniform over 8 ns. Mean loss and spread: 1.4% and 1.2% at 25 ns, 0.4% and 0.3% at 87.5 ns, 2.6% and 2.3% at 12.5 ns. Negligible against the 0.1 pe noise except at the shortest shaping.

Crosstalk. Internal crosstalk is simultaneous and gives the 2–3 pe rungs; it is what the ladder uses. Optical crosstalk between neighbouring SiPMs without a crystal is small and simultaneous, so a ToA selection treats both channels as at-peak; harmless. No issue specific to either mode.

Is peak stretch better for the SPE gain? For the gain, yes, in this respect: the peak detector stores the true maximum of every pulse whose peak falls inside the window, whatever its crossing time, so it has no walk and needs no own-trigger selection. With a periodic trigger (PTRG) and a 300 ns window, 73% of events contain at least one dark pulse per channel, and all 64 channels give a 0-1-2-3 pe ladder in one run. Its costs are a pedestal shifted by the noise maximum (next section), a common offset that cancels in the slope and is already the reason the design fits the energy scale through two peaks (fullsim_design.tex:1003–1004), and a high-side tail from the tails of earlier pulses.

## 2. Claims (a), (b), (c) and the meaning of HoldDelay (question 2)

Datasheet facts used. Two sampling systems per shaper, chosen by bits 306/307 (datasheet:673–680). The peak detector has three phases: track before any trigger "to avoid noise pile-up", peak detection from the trigger, hold from the rising edge of hold_xg (datasheet:700–712). With FERSlib's external arming the window is from gtrg (chip pin global_trig, FERS_configure_5202.c:246) to gtrg + HoldDelay, as the rationale and the simulation (citiroc.jl:220–224, k0 = trigger) assume. The datasheet itself recommends T&H for simultaneous photons and the peak detector for photons spread over tens to hundreds of ns (datasheet:684–685), which is the energy-run case; for a single dark avalanche both apply.

(a) "A window of a few hundred ns often contains a second dark pulse, and the maximum picks it up." The first half is true (73% for 300 ns at 4.4 MHz; 36% for 100 ns), the second is wrong in mechanism. The peak detector stores the maximum of the summed waveform: two 1 pe pulses farther apart than the pulse width give 1 pe plus the tail of the earlier one at the time of the later peak, not 2 pe. The sum appears only when the second pulse overlaps the first within about its FWHM (58 ns at 25 ns shaping, 117 ns at 87.5 ns), with probability 23% and 40%. In T&H a second pulse adds more than 0.1 pe to the held value when it falls within 96 ns (25 ns shaping) or 181 ns (87.5 ns) of the hold: 34% and 55%. Pile-up onto the stored value is therefore of the same order in both modes, slightly worse in T&H at long shaping; the difference is the high-side tail from non-overlapping pulses in the peak detector. The sentence should be rewritten.

(b) "The maximum of the noise over a window is not the noise of one sample." Correct, and the strongest argument. For shaped white noise the maximum over 300 ns is +2.2σ with a spread of 0.48σ at 25 ns shaping, +1.7σ with 0.65σ at 87.5 ns (100 ns window: +1.7σ/0.60σ and +0.9σ/0.81σ). So the peak-detector pedestal is biased up and narrower than σ, not wider. The design models exactly this and tests it (fullsim_design.tex:596–602, 1066–1068), so the PD pedestal width can be inverted to σ, but only under the assumed noise spectrum; T&H measures σ with no assumption. Note also that the 1, 2, 3 pe peaks in PD mode are biased less than the pedestal (the signal is flat only for a few ns around its peak, so the maximum sees fewer independent noise samples, roughly +0.7σ), hence a ladder intercept that differs from the measured pedestal by about one σ (0.1 pe). This is worth stating since it is the reason the pedestal cannot serve as the zero of the PD ladder.

(c) "Scanning the hold delay in T&H maps the shaped pulse point by point." True for the channel that produced the trigger, convolved with the 8 ns clock phase and the walk; false for the other 63 channels, whose scan is flat. The DAQ scan runs with the plan's TriggerMask (backend/internal/acquisition/hold_delay.go:78) and the production plan triggers on MAJ64 with majority 4 (test/fixtures/janus/config_same4_v3_good.txt:66, 74, 76), which with SiPMs alone is a random four-fold dark coincidence; the scan request has no trigger field, so the plan must be changed for the calibration. In peak-detector mode the same scan maps the rising edge and then a plateau, enough to locate the peak (the knee), not the falling edge. The rationale's hardware test (lines 134–138) is correct.

HoldDelay meaning. Consistent: FERSlib writes HoldDelay/8 ns into a_hold_delay "Time from gtrg to peak hold" (FERS_configure_5202.c:254; FERS_Registers_520X.h:84; Janus_Config_Original.txt:110 "delay between bunch trigger (start of peak detection) and hold (stop of peak detection)"); the DAQ writes delay/8 and reports readback × 8 (holddelay/scan.go:116–119, 152), with 8 ns steps enforced (scan.go:52). What the rationale leaves out is that gtrg is the discriminator crossing plus a fixed FPGA latency L that nobody has measured. The scan measures (peak delay − L), and the model peak is only 31 ns after the crossing at 25 ns shaping (58 ns at 50 ns, 90 ns at 87.5 ns). If L exceeds 31 ns, neither mode can reach the peak at 25 ns shaping: T&H samples the falling edge, and the peak detector, armed after the peak, stores the value at arming. The v1 note flagged this (bench/v1/2_citiroc_hold_track.tex:165–172); the rationale and the v2 bench note should keep it, and the first scan should be at 87.5 ns.

## 3. The simulation's idealisation (question 3)

The event path models the common hold correctly: one trigger time per event, maximum from trigger to hold or value at the hold for every channel (citiroc.jl:210–245), and "hits" defined by a crossing within the coincidence window of the trigger (citiroc.jl:241), i.e. a ToA-like selection. The calibration path is different: hg_at_crossings holds each channel at each of its own crossings, T&H only (peak_detector = false hard-coded, citiroc.jl:277–283), with the hold at the delay that maximises a single clean avalanche (hold_at_peak, citiroc.jl:290–296; calibration.jl:68, 81). The design states the idealisation (fullsim_design.tex:1213–1218).

Consequences for the comparison of modes. (i) The simulated calibration has no peak-detector branch, so the simulation cannot yet say which mode calibrates better; the comparison in the rationale rests on the toy (hold_scan_figs.py:78–104), which also holds each pulse at its own crossing plus 1 ns jitter and has no common hold, no clock phase, no latency. (ii) The simulated T&H selection is the ideal ToA selection, not the hit-register flag the DAQ will store; the bench spectra of PR #75 and the simulated ones are not the same observable. (iii) The idealisation already contains the walk bias (its crossings are those of the real fast-shaped pulses), so the 3.9%/1% slope bias will appear in both, but the design sentence "the shaping is short enough for the dark pulses not to pile up under the held value" (fullsim_design.tex:933–934) is not supported: 34% of T&H samples at 25 ns shaping carry more than 0.1 pe from another pulse. (iv) The low-gain zero of the design, the mean LG of channels "whose discriminator did not fire" in self-triggered events (fullsim_design.tex:607–609; calibration.jl:94–99), uses the simulated hit (crossing within the coincidence window); on the board it would use the hit-register flag, whose gate is undefined, so that zero is not yet a reproducible observable either. None of this changes the energy-run conclusions, which use the common-hold path; it does mean that the calibration comparison between modes is open until the simulation gets a peak-detector calibration branch and the DAQ keeps the ToA.

## 4. What is missing (question 4)

1. Pedestal with PTRG in both modes, below breakdown, at the three shapings. T&H gives σ; the peak detector gives the biased maximum; their ratio tests the shaped-white-noise assumption that the simulation uses to convert one into the other. The v2 bench note has only the peak-detector version (bench/1_citiroc_noise_v2.tex:134–138).

2. Gain versus mode and versus hold delay. The 1 pe position in T&H at its best delay against the peak-detector plateau for the same channel and shaping; expected equal within the walk (≤ 2% at 25 ns) plus the +0.7σ noise-maximum offset common to all PD peaks. A larger difference points at peak-detector droop or tracking limits, which the design lists as unknown (fullsim_design.tex:1202–1204) and which decide whether the energy-run gain can be taken from a T&H calibration at all.

3. Statistics with a common self trigger. The OR of 64 channels gives each channel about 1/64 of the events; the A5202 converts 64 channels through a 300 ns multiplexer (Janus_Config_Original.txt:111), so the rate saturates around a few tens of kHz, and the DAQ caps a scan point at 100 000 events (holddelay/scan.go:55): about 1 500 own-trigger events per channel, 300 at 2 pe and 60 at 3 pe. Restricting the trigger logic to one channel or a group of eight with Tlogic_Mask0/1 (Janus_Config_Original.txt:92–93) multiplies this by 8 to 64 and makes every event a peak sample for the masked channel; eight runs cover the board.

4. Charge injection. FERSlib exposes the internal test pulse into the preamplifiers (TestPulseSource, TestPulseAmplitude, TestPulsePreamp; FERS_configure_5202.c:188–200) with deterministic timing from PTRG. It maps the shaper in T&H with no dark-pulse statistics, measures L and the peak-detector linearity against T&H directly, and separates chip from sensor. Whether the DAQ catalogue passes these settings should be checked; LED and ToT bring nothing the dark pulses do not already give.

5. The gate of the hit register. One PTRG run above breakdown gives the fraction of flagged channels per event, hence the effective gate width W from 1 − exp(−r W). Until W is known, any selection or zero built on the flag (PR #75, the design's LG zero) is unspecified.

6. The thresholds. The staircase writes the same value to both coarse thresholds (backend/internal/staircase/scan.go:110), while the production file has 250 and 183 (fixture lines 90, 95); the flag follows the charge threshold, the trigger the time threshold. The calibration configuration must state both, and note that FastShaperInput is LG-PA in production (fixture line 89) and HG-PA in the SPE plan (v1 note line 258), which changes the threshold scale.

## 5. Verdict and what to do first (question 5)

Not justified as written. Qualified statement that the evidence supports: T&H is the better mode to understand the chain, because it maps the shaped pulse of the triggering channel and measures the single-sample noise directly; the peak detector is the better, or at least the simpler and less biased, mode for the SPE gain of all channels at once, because it is insensitive to the trigger phase and to the time walk and works with a periodic trigger; both are needed, and the gains from both should agree within the walk correction. Claim (a) should be replaced by the pile-up comparison above, claim (c) restricted to the triggering channel with the ToA selection named as its condition, and the unknown trigger-to-hold latency added.

On the DAQ side, PR #75 should store, per scan point, either the per-event energies with their timings or at least spectra split by ToA class (earliest channel within ±3 ns, others), and the scan should accept a trigger mask; the hit-register flag can stay as a secondary column once its gate is measured.

Order for the bench: first the PTRG runs below breakdown in both modes (pedestal, two hours of work, no DAQ change needed for the peak detector); second a PTRG run above breakdown in peak-detector mode, which already yields the 0-1-2-3 ladder of all 64 channels and the flag occupancy; third, once #74 is on the laboratory machine, a T&H hold scan at 87.5 ns with the trigger logic masked to one channel, in SPECT_TIMING, which measures L, the pulse map and the T&H gain to compare with the second step. The 25 ns scan comes after L is known.

## Addendum: is track and hold worth it?

Cost-benefit judgment, asked by the author after the review. The physics goal is the 511 keV energy resolution of BGO and CsI(Tl) read in peak-stretch mode, and the validation of the simulation chain against the bench.

### What each T&H measurement changes

**Noise (benefit 1): nothing that we will use.** The quantity that enters the energy runs is the peak-stretch pedestal itself, mean shift and width per channel on both gains, and PTRG runs below breakdown in peak-stretch mode measure it directly, in the mode and with the window of the energy runs. The simulation already produces the same observable (fullsim_design.tex:596–602, validated at 1066–1068), so the comparison is one-to-one without a single-sample σ. The three shapings and the two hold delays of the v2 bench note give six pedestal moments against a one-parameter noise model plus an assumed spectrum, which is more than enough to catch a wrong spectrum. How much the number matters: on the high gain, 0.1 pe rms per channel summed over 64 channels is 0.8 pe against a photon-statistics width of about 30 pe for 930 stored photoelectrons (BGO, 2800 pe × 0.33), negligible even if the true noise is seven times larger. On the low gain, where the energy is read, the same noise is 1 pe per channel, 8 pe summed, a 3.5% increase of the statistical width; at the datasheet bound of 0.7 pe it would be 56 pe and dominate. So the low-gain peak-stretch pedestal must be measured, and T&H adds nothing to that.

**Pulse shape and latency (benefit 2): the one number that moves.** The stored fraction (0.33 for BGO at room temperature, 0.10 for CsI(Tl), 0.12 for cold BGO; fullsim_design.tex:627–655) is set by the effective window ∫h dt / h_peak of the single-avalanche response, 115 ns in the current model against 280 ns for the model the note replaced. It fixes the photoelectron scale of the 511 keV peak and the statistical term of the resolution, which scales as (f N)^(-1/2): a 20% error in the window is a 20% error in the predicted peak position and 10% in the predicted statistical resolution. A peak-stretch hold scan maps only the rising edge and then a plateau; the window needs the fall and the undershoot. The internal test pulse maps the shaper but not the convolution with the 2 nF SiPM pulse, which is the uncertain part; the fast-shaper ToT says nothing about the slow shaper. There are two bench measurements of the full shape:

- The analog probe. The A5202 exposes SLOW_HG, SLOW_LG, FAST and the preamplifier outputs of one selectable channel on its analog probe (Janus_Config_Original.txt:121–124; FERSlib ConfigureProbe5202), and the DAQ catalogue already accepts AnalogProbe0/1 and ProbeChannel0 (backend/internal/janusconfig/catalog.go:53–54). An oscilloscope on the probe with the SiPMs dark gives the single-avalanche shaped waveform, its peaking time, fall and undershoot, in an afternoon, with no DAQ change, no mode change and no statistics problem. This is the measurement the design note itself asks for (fullsim_design.tex:559–561). Its caveat is that the probe buffer may distort the waveform (bandwidth, loading), which a comparison of its peaking time with the hold scan settles.
- T&H with a periodic trigger. One ordinary data run per shaping with PeakDetectorMode = TRACK&HOLD and BunchTrgSource = PTRG, above breakdown, with no event selection at all: the value held at a random phase of a sparse dark train (occupancy about 25% at 4.4 MHz and 58 ns FWHM) has the distribution of the time the response spends at each level, including the negative undershoot, so the per-channel amplitude spectrum is a shape-sensitive observable of h(t) for all 64 channels at once. The simulation produces exactly the same observable through its event path with trigger "external" and peak_detector false (citiroc.jl:210–245), with no change to the calibration path. This needs #73 and #74 only.

Latency L: needed only to know whether the 25 ns shaping is reachable; it falls out of any T&H scan and also of the test pulse. It changes no physics number for the energy runs, which use 87.5 ns and a 300 ns hold.

**SPE gain (benefit 3): no number changes.** The gain that the energy runs need is the peak-stretch gain, which a PTRG run above breakdown gives for all channels with no selection and no walk bias. A T&H gain would only cross-check the peak detector's linearity between 1 and 3 pe, far from the hundreds of photoelectrons per channel of the energy runs.

**Anything else (benefit 4).** The hit-register gate and the low-gain zero of the design are peak-stretch questions; the simulation of both modes in the calibration path serves only a comparison we no longer need if the gain is taken in peak-stretch mode.

### Recommendation

Keep a minimal T&H check; do not do the full programme. The full programme (ToA classes in #75, a trigger-mask PR, scans per channel group, a second calibration branch in the simulation, a bench round of 30 to 50 runs for Laura) buys a per-channel pulse map and a gain cross-check whose effect on the 511 keV numbers is below the other uncertainties. What is worth having is the single-avalanche shape, and the cheapest route to it is the analog probe, with T&H as the 64-channel confirmation. Concretely:

1. Oscilloscope on the analog probe, SLOW_HG and FAST of two or three channels, at 87.5 and 25 ns shaping, SiPMs dark at the working overvoltage. Half a day, no DAQ change. Gives the response shape and the peaking time; the simulation's pulse model is fitted to it and the stored fractions recomputed.
2. Peak-stretch calibration as planned in the v2 bench note (PTRG below breakdown for the pedestals on both gains; PTRG above breakdown for the SPE ladder of all channels; the peak-stretch hold scan to locate the plateau). This is the calibration the energy runs use.
3. Minimal T&H check, once #73 and #74 run on the laboratory PC: three ordinary data runs, PTRG, TRACK&HOLD, HoldDelay at the plateau found in step 2, one per shaping (87.5, 50, 25 ns), 100 000 events each, a few minutes of bench time. Compare the per-channel spectra with the simulation's external-trigger T&H output. If a point-by-point map is still wanted later, it costs no DAQ change either: a series of SPECT_TIMING data runs at different HoldDelay, trigger T-OR or TLOGIC with Tlogic_Mask0/1 restricted to one channel group (both masks are accepted hardware settings, catalog.go:45), the earliest-ToA channel selected offline from the per-event timing rows that data runs already store (hdf5store/writer.go:408, 739).

Drop T&H entirely only if step 1 shows the probe is clean and the shape agrees with the model; then step 3 is a confirmation one can skip.

### Minimum DAQ change set

#73 is needed regardless of T&H, because without it FastShaperInput and HV_Adjust_Range do not reach the chip. #74 alone is enough for the recommended check: a data run with PeakDetectorMode is an ordinary run with full-resolution per-event energies and, in SPECT_TIMING, per-channel ToA. #75 is not needed for calibration; its scan is a convenience to find the plateau, and its hit-register split should not be used for selection. No trigger-mask PR is needed, since Tlogic_Mask0/1 are configuration settings. The simulation needs no new calibration branch for the check; the event path already models both modes under an external trigger. The second calibration branch (peak_detector in hg_at_crossings) is worth adding only because the bench SPE gain will be a peak-stretch gain and the simulated calibration should be the same measurement; that is a small change independent of T&H.
