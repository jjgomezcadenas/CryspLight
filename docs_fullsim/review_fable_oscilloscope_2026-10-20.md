Date: 2026-10-20. Section reviewed: "La forma del pulso: osciloscopio" (lines 56–95). ## Summary

The procedure measures the right signal and is nearly sufficient. Four things need changing before a student runs it: the window is too short for the undershoot (the model settles only 750 ns after the trigger at 87.5 ns), the baseline of a threshold-triggered average is biased low by up to a few tenths of a photoelectron at 4 MHz and needs a random-trigger zero line, the DAQ run as configured in note 1 stops after 10 s, and the low-gain probe at 1 pe is below the scope noise and needs a different trigger. Two additions cost almost nothing and remove the two biggest uncertainties of the Citiroc model: PREAMP_HG (the 2 nF SiPM pulse itself) and FAST (the trigger-time reference), and a repeat at 3 V overvoltage.

## 1. Does it give what the simulation needs?

Yes, for the shape. The probe taps Out_ssh_HG / Out_ssh_LG / Out_fs / Out_PA (datasheet 1024–1038), the shaper and preamplifier outputs themselves, before the track-and-hold or peak-detector cell, so the hold is indeed irrelevant and removing it from the procedure was right. What the simulation uses is h(t) normalised to its peak with a known zero, over the full response including the undershoot: in the toy model the undershoot is −0.46 of the peak at 324 ns for 87.5 ns shaping (−0.39 at 242 ns for 50 ns, −0.30 at 184 ns for 25 ns) and |h| falls below 2% of the peak only after 750, 616 and 522 ns. The note's 500 ns window (line 80) therefore cuts the undershoot at 87.5 ns. Use a 2 µs record (200 ns/div) with the trigger at 30% for every setting; the fine structure of the 25 ns pulse is still resolved at 1 GS/s.

Form for the analysis: time zero should be defined in the analysis by the peak (or the half-maximum of the rising edge), not by the trigger point, which is a threshold crossing with walk; normalisation by the peak of the zero-subtracted average; the absolute mV/pe is a secondary output (the ADC scale is calibrated by the SPE ladder). The zero line is the problem, treated next. One more caveat: the datasheet says the probe output "should be disabled by Slow Control in normal use" (line 1021) and has its own OTA enable, stream bit 1131; the DAQ's planned stream leaves PowerEnableProbeOTA false (config.go:617 does not set it; citiroc.go:169), but the chip's real stream is built by the firmware from the probe register, so whether the FPGA turns the OTA on is not known from the sources. If no signal appears on the MCX, this bit is the first suspect, and a readback of the stream (VerifyCitirocReadback) would show it.

## 2. Pile-up and averaging

At 4.4 MHz a 2 µs record holds about nine other pulses, and that is fine for the shape as long as they are uncorrelated with the trigger: the mean of a Poisson train of pulses is constant in time, so uncorrelated pulses add a constant to the average and not a distortion. Averaging 1000 triggers does not bias the shape through pile-up; the noise after 1000 averages is 0.003 pe (model σ 0.1 pe), far below the baseline problem.

The bias comes from the trigger. The chain is AC coupled (the chip removes the mean of the dark current), so the train has zero mean: the positive lobes of the pulses are balanced by their undershoots, and the mean dark level removed is r⟨n⟩ × (positive-lobe area), about 4.4 MHz × 1.2 × 125 ns·pe ≈ 0.6 pe at 87.5 ns shaping (0.3 pe at 25 ns). A threshold-triggered record contains, before the trigger, no pulse above threshold since the scope re-armed (it would have triggered earlier), but it does contain the undershoots of earlier pulses: the pre-trigger baseline sits below the true zero by a fraction of that 0.6 pe that depends on the scope's re-arm time, typically one to a few tenths of a photoelectron. Measured against that baseline the undershoot comes out too shallow and the "effective window" too long. The same holds for an average of selected clean singles, and for a trigger from the chip discriminator. What to do, in order of value:

1. Record a zero line for every setting: the same average with the scope triggered on something uncorrelated with the pulses, T0-OUT with T0_Out = PTRG (an accepted setting, catalog.go:41; config.go:541 writes it) on a second scope channel, or the scope's own auto/line trigger. Its mean is the true zero (plus the DC offset of the probe path). Subtract it from the pulse-triggered average instead of a pre-trigger baseline. This is one extra averaged file per setting.
2. Keep 600 ns of pre-trigger anyway (it shows the depletion and the noise) and set the trigger holdoff longer than the record so records do not overlap.
3. Repeat at 3 V overvoltage: the dark rate and the crosstalk roughly halve, so the bias halves while the normalised shape must not change; a shape that depends on overvoltage after zero subtraction points at the baseline treatment, not at the chip.
4. Compare like with like: the simulation can reproduce the scope procedure exactly (threshold trigger on the shaped dark train, re-arm, averaging, random-trigger zero). A tracked script producing the simulated average is the safe comparison; extracting h(t) from the data and comparing it with the bare model response is the second-best.

Rejecting piled-up singles is not needed and not efficient: a single shot clean within ±300 ns has probability exp(−2.6) ≈ 7%, so 1000 shots give about 70, with noise 0.012 pe; the average already does better, and both share the baseline bias. The dozen singles in the note (line 86–87) remain useful for the pulse height, the noise and a look at pile-up; if the scope has sequence memory, a few hundred singles in one file are better than a dozen CSVs.

## 3. Trigger at half the 1 pe amplitude

Right, with two remarks. Noise: at the model noise (0.1 pe) half a photoelectron is 5σ and the noise crossing rate is 10–25 Hz against 4.4 MHz of pulses; even at 3σ it would be 20–70 kHz, still 1% of the pulses. Do not go below 0.4 pe. Crosstalk: about 20% of triggers are 2–3 pe pulses, same shape if the chain is linear, which cross the threshold 5–6 ns earlier (model) and so enter the average shifted; the normalised average moves by about 1 ns and broadens by less than 1% at 87.5 ns, 1–2% at 25 ns. Acceptable; if the scope has a window (runt) trigger, bounds of 0.5 and 1.5 pe remove it. Set the slope and polarity after looking at a single pulse (the datasheet does not state the probe polarity), DC coupling, and log the trigger level in mV.

A cleaner trigger for everything, including the low gain (section 4), is the chip's own discriminator: Tlogic_Mask0/1 restricted to the probed channel and T0_Out = T-OR on the LEMO T0-OUT, both accepted settings (catalog.go:45, 41). It triggers at the configured TD threshold like the DAQ does, has no walk problem different from the DAQ's, and the delay from its edge to the shaper peak is the chip's part of the hold delay, a number worth logging. It has the same baseline depletion as any pulse trigger, so item 1 of section 2 still applies.

## 4. Amplitude, scale, bandwidth, termination, averages

Expected 1 pe amplitude at SLOW_HG. HG_Gain = 55 means Cf = (63 − 55) × 25 fF = 200 fF and a voltage gain Cin/Cf = 15 pF/0.2 pF = 75 (datasheet 600–608); LG 7.5. At 5 V overvoltage the S14160 gain is about 4.6 × 10⁶ (2.5 × 10⁶ at 2.7 V), 0.74 pC per avalanche; the model pulse has a peak current of 3.7 µA, 0.19 mV across 50 Ω, 14 mV after the HG preamplifier, and the shaper keeps 0.26, 0.24 and 0.19 of that at 87.5, 50 and 25 ns: 3–4 mV per photoelectron. The board's SPE spacing (tens of ADC codes over a ~2 V range) suggests closer to 10 mV, so expect 3–10 mV on SLOW_HG; the probe OTA and the board driver gains are unknown. Vertical scale 5–10 mV/div, DC coupling, no attenuating probe. SLOW_LG at 87.5 ns is then 0.3–1 mV per photoelectron, below the noise of most scopes at 1 MΩ: triggering at half a photoelectron is impossible there. Options: trigger on T0-OUT = T-OR of the probed channel (section 3) and average; or trigger at the 2–3 pe level, where the shape is the same; or raise LG_Gain to 62 (Cf 25 fF, gain 60, eight times more) for the LG shape runs only, noting that Cf also changes the preamplifier response slightly. The first is the cleanest.

Bandwidth. The 25 ns shaper has θ = 12.5 ns and the fast shaper 7.5 ns: a 20 MHz bandwidth limit (8 ns time constant) distorts both; keep the limit off and use a ≥ 100 MHz scope. At 1 MΩ the load is the scope's ~15 pF plus about 100 pF per metre of RG174; with a 50 Ω source that is a 6 ns time constant per metre, 5% on the 25 ns peak time and more on FAST. Use the shortest cable available, half a metre or less, and log its length.

Termination. The manual's 1 MΩ is right only if the board back-terminates the probe driver; otherwise an open line rings with the round-trip time, 10 ns per metre of RG174, visible at 25 ns and on FAST. One check at the start: record the same pulse at 1 MΩ and at 50 Ω. If 50 Ω halves the amplitude and keeps the shape, the driver is back-terminated and 50 Ω is the cleaner choice for the rest; if 50 Ω collapses or distorts the pulse, stay at 1 MΩ with the short cable.

Averages. 1000 are enough for the statistics (0.3% of the peak per point); 4000 cost nothing extra at 4 MHz and help FAST and the low gain. Store the time column, the sample interval and the number of averages with each CSV, save the oscilloscope setup file once, and a screenshot per setting.

## 5. Missing or wrong for the student

- The run stops. Note 1 sets PRESET_COUNTS = 10 000 at PtrgPeriod 1 ms: the run, which only serves to apply the configuration and keep the bias on, ends after 10 s. Set PresetCounts to a very large number or stop the run by hand, and say so.
- Settings to state. HV_Vbias for 5 V overvoltage (about 43 V at room temperature with the current HV_IndivAdj, as in the v1 SPE note), HG_Gain and LG_Gain as in the production configuration (55), FastShaperInput = HG-PA if FAST is recorded (production has LG-PA), the TD threshold if T0-OUT = T-OR is used, AcquisitionMode SPECTROSCOPY, everything else as in note 1. The note says "5 V de overvoltage" without the HV value and leaves the gains implicit.
- Two more probe points. PREAMP_HG at one shaping (it does not depend on the shaper): it is the SiPM pulse with 2 nF into the chip's input, the single most uncertain input of the model (fullsim_design.tex, open items), and it decides between the 69 ns-peak pulse of the model and a faster one. FAST at one shaping: the trigger reference of the chain and a check of the probe bandwidth (model peak 31 ns after the avalanche). One averaged file each, plus their zero lines.
- Two overvoltages, 5 and 3 V: separates baseline and pile-up effects from the chip (section 2) and checks linearity of the shape; the SiPM pulse shape itself does not change with overvoltage, its amplitude does.
- Two channels, one near the centre and one at the edge of the array: the shaper is common to the chip, the preamplifiers are not; a second channel at one shaping is enough.
- The logbook table lacks the oscilloscope columns: probe signal, channel, termination, cable length, vertical scale, trigger source and level, number of averages, record length and sample interval. Add them for the CSV rows.
- AnalogProbe0 = OFF at the end is in the note; add that the probe run configuration must not be reused for the DAQ calibration runs because of the gain change the manual mentions.

## Corrected procedure, minimal

Per setting (SLOW_HG at 87.5, 50, 25 ns; SLOW_LG at 87.5 ns; FAST and PREAMP_HG at 87.5 ns), at 5 V and then at 3 V overvoltage, channel A, with channel B at 87.5 ns only:

1. DAQ: configuration of note 1 with HV for the overvoltage, AnalogProbe0 = signal, ProbeChannel0 = channel, T0_Out = PTRG (or T-OR with Tlogic_Mask on the channel), PresetCounts very large. Start the run.
2. Scope: ≥ 100 MHz, bandwidth limit off, DC, 1 MΩ or 50 Ω after the one-time check, cable ≤ 0.5 m, 2 µs record, trigger at 30%, holdoff > 2 µs. Trigger on the probe at half a photoelectron, rising slope (or on T0-OUT).
3. Save: average of ≥ 1000 (pulse trigger); average of ≥ 1000 with the trigger on T0-OUT = PTRG or auto (zero line); 10–20 singles or a sequence file. Name files with probe, channel, shaping, overvoltage, termination.
4. Analysis: subtract the zero line, align on the peak, normalise to the peak, and compare with the simulated scope average produced by a tracked script with the same trigger and window.
