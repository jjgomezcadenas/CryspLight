The new BGO material settings are used by the reported runs, and Gironnet's equation reproduces the adopted 0.84 µs at 250 K. The light-yield digitisation nevertheless fails its own stated normalisation check.
The ASIC noise justification contains a factor-of-ten error. The assumed noise remains possible, but calling it conservative is unsupported, and a larger allowed noise changes the calibration result substantially.
The Radioroc population near zero and most of the cold digitiser gain bias have identifiable causes in the calibration implementation; neither requires an unexplained hardware effect.
The cold digitiser threshold and the ASIC calibration depend materially on alternatives to the dark-rate law, input termination and local-channel acquisition model.
The energy-resolution values are conditional simulation results. The small decomposition terms are unresolved; the larger BGO improvement is also present outside the Gaussian peak fit and is not, by itself, evidence of a fitter defect.

## Verified defects

### 1. The Gironnet digitisation does not satisfy its claimed normalisation check

**Where:** scripts/bgo_gironnet_table.py, lines 9–23; data/bgo_gironnet_T.csv; papers/tau_dependence_BGO.txt, section 3.

**What is wrong and evidence:** The script says that the low-temperature plateau is 56 and that normalising to 23,700 ph/MeV at 6 K agrees within 1% with normalising to 6,900 ph/MeV at 295 K. Its actual low-temperature points are approximately 50.3, and its interpolated value at 295 K is 16.4. Their ratio is 3.067, which gives approximately 21,163 ph/MeV at the plateau when anchored to 6,900. Conversely, anchoring that plateau to 23,700 gives about 7,727 ph/MeV at 295 K, 12% above 6,900. The claimed 1% agreement is false for the stored array.

The same array places 250 K at 55.1% of its plateau. The paper's text says half the maximum at 250 K and approximately a factor 3.5 between the plateau and room temperature. Those textual anchors imply a 250 K/295 K ratio near 1.75, compared with the array's 1.689. This is a consistency check against the text, not an independent reading of graphical coordinates: I followed the instruction to use the text rather than the PDF.

**Consequence:** The adopted 13,500 ph/MeV is consistent with the stored ratio scaled to 8,000, but its provenance is less precise than the script claims. The textual anchors would suggest approximately 14,000, about 3.7% higher. This does not establish that 14,000 is the correct replacement: the paper's approximate statements and yield uncertainty must be retained. The CSV's absolute yields also remain on the explicitly stated 6,900 reference, rather than the material files' 8,000 reference; users must rescale them.

The decay calculation itself checks: Eq. (1) gives 0.4366 µs at 295 K, 2.075 µs at 200 K and 0.8406 µs at 250 K. The first two agree with the quoted 0.43 ± 0.08 and 2.2 ± 0.2 µs measurements. The warning about sub-microsecond precision is appropriate. The paper explicitly motivates using the alpha-excitation temperature dependence for gamma yield, including supporting measurements over 253–350 K; this is a reasonable nearby extrapolation to 250 K, rather than a demonstrated exact conversion.

**Proposed fix:** Reconcile the digitised plateau, its documentation and the normalisation check; expose the adopted reference yield explicitly in the generated table. Attach a digitisation uncertainty to the ratio instead of presenting the inconsistent anchor agreement as validation. Evidence is reproduced in analytic.log.

### 2. The claimed conservative ASIC noise follows from a factor-of-ten arithmetic error

**Where:** docs_fullsim/fullsim_design.tex, lines 543–554 and 760–764; docs_fullsim/room_temperature_study.tex, noise description in section 3; papers/radioroc2-datasheet.txt, lines 8–13.

**What is wrong and evidence:** A signal-to-noise ratio above 10 for a 160 fC signal implies noise below 16 fC, not below 1.6 fC. Even granting the document's association with 100 pF and its linear capacitance extrapolation, multiplying by 20 gives an upper scale of 320 fC. At a SiPM gain of 2.79 million electrons, that is 0.716 photoelectron, rather than 0.07. Moreover, the datasheet attaches the 100 pF condition explicitly to its timing statement; it does not supply a complete noise-versus-capacitance-and-shaping specification.

The bound is not a measurement of 0.716 photoelectron. Noise of 0.1 may still be correct. What fails is the numerical argument that it is conservative. A scratch sensitivity test kept the primary dark rate, pulse, time discriminator and 100 ns high-gain shaping fixed and changed only high-gain noise from 0.1 to 0.7 photoelectron, with the existing low-gain noise scaling. In 2,000 single-channel room-temperature dark records, the ladder fit returned 9.83 versus 5.90 ADC counts per cell for a true gain of 10. Both were finite fits; the latter is a wrong calibration, not a reported fit failure. The diagnostic used a 2 ns synthesis grid and the same ADC rounding and uniform count spreading as the calibration procedure.

**Consequence:** The evidence for calibrating all channels at the proposed short shaping depends on an optimistic noise assumption. Merely counting finite ladder fits does not establish successful calibration when the peaks become poorly resolved. The calculation does not justify replacing the production noise by 0.7; it demonstrates the consequential range left open by the corrected bound.

**Proposed fix:** Correct the arithmetic and remove the conservative designation. Keep the noise explicitly assumed until measured, test calibration across a defensible range, and reject ladders lacking convincing peak-spacing evidence. Re-evaluate the shaping choice with the measured noise. Evidence: basic_checks.jl/basic_checks.log and analytic.log.

### 3. The Radioroc population labelled zero includes real one-cell pulses, and the ladder can start one cell too high

**Where:** src/frontend/shaping.jl, lines 49–55; src/frontend/radioroc.jl, radioroc_waves; fullsim_analysis/lib/fits.jl, lines 145–153; fullsim_analysis/scripts/calibration.jl, line 142; room_temperature_study.tex, lines 419–423.

**What is wrong and evidence:** The note interprets the near-zero population as time-discriminator crossings without a high-gain signal. The implementation removes the ensemble mean dark charge before shaping. At room temperature, 3 V and 100 ns shaping, this subtracts a steady high-gain level of 1.47361 cell equivalents. An isolated one-cell avalanche therefore has a real high-gain maximum at −0.47361 relative to the electronic pedestal. With pedestal 200 and gain 10, the noiseless held values for one, two and three simultaneous cells are 195.264, 205.264 and 215.264 ADC counts. All three have valid time-discriminator crossings.

The ladder is told that the pedestal is 200 and chooses its first photoelectron peak only above pedestal plus 0.3 spacings. It can consequently discard the physical one-cell peak and call the two-cell peak “one”. The saved room-temperature BGO calibration has median fitted zero 196.24 counts, close to the one-cell value above. Plotting amplitudes relative to that fitted zero puts real one-cell events near zero. At 250 K the subtraction is only 0.05310 cell equivalents: the one-cell held value is 209.469 counts, safely above the ladder's cutoff, explaining why the extra near-zero population disappears.

These controlled examples use the implemented dark-level subtraction and an otherwise isolated pulse; they demonstrate that an absent avalanche or failed high-gain response is unnecessary. Pile-up and noise broaden the production distributions.

**Consequence:** The calibration figure's multiplicity labels and fitted high-gain zero are not reliable at room temperature. An integer relabelling alone would leave the slope unchanged for a perfect ladder, but this spectrum is not a perfect ladder: selecting different rungs also changes which pile-up-distorted peaks determine the slope. The low-gain energy analysis does not use this high-gain intercept directly, so this finding is not a claim of a one-photoelectron offset in every physics channel.

**Proposed fix:** Model or estimate the dark-dependent baseline when assigning multiplicities; do not use the electronic pedestal as the first-rung cutoff. Validate assignments against labelled simulated clusters, including isolated one-cell pulses embedded in stationary dark histories, and then refit the gains and zeros. Correct the explanation of the near-zero population. Evidence: zero_population.jl/zero_population.log.

### 4. The digitiser matched filter is normalised at a different alignment from the maximum it measures

**Where:** fullsim_analysis/scripts/calibration.jl, lines 98–102 and 116–121; fullsim_analysis/lib/calibrate.jl, digitiser branch; room_temperature_study.tex, digitiser calibration results.

**What is wrong and evidence:** The template normalisation makes the output equal to the pulse amplitude at the template's reference alignment. The actual estimator searches local maxima after subtracting four preceding samples. With the present long pulse and a template containing only ten samples, its maximum occurs at another alignment. Running the exact filter on a single noiseless unit pulse gives maxima of 1.06501, 1.05126, 1.04373 and 1.05447 for avalanche phases 0, 2, 4 and 6 ns. The maximum is displaced by 6–12 ns from the avalanche. Thus a roughly 5% excess exists without noise or pile-up; it closely explains the reported cold fitted/true ratio of 1.056.

Dark pulses in the preceding baseline and in the template window change this estimator, so changing their rate need not preserve that isolated-pulse excess. The warm ratio of 1.031 is not evidence that the hardware's gain changed. The isolated-pulse calculation identifies the main cold bias; it does not separately quantify every pile-up contribution to the warm fit.

**Consequence:** measured_charge divides integrated charge by the fitted matched-filter amplitude times the physical pulse's area/peak ratio. That conversion assumes the estimator is an unbiased measurement of peak height. It therefore undercounts photoelectron equivalents by about 5% in the cold case. The different quoted biases alone suppress a cold/warm recorded-charge ratio by 1.031/1.056 = 0.976, about 2.4%. The two-line energy calibration absorbs a common gain factor within a run; it does not correct this light-yield comparison.

**Proposed fix:** Calibrate the complete amplitude estimator, including its maximum search and sampling phase, against isolated pulses; or calibrate the single-cell area with the same charge estimator used for physics data. Then quantify residual dark-rate dependence. Add an isolated-pulse regression check over the sampling phases. Evidence: the MATCH outputs in basic_checks.log.

### 5. The stated light-budget corrections do not match the implemented scintillation and crosstalk models

**Where:** fullsim_design.tex, lines 66–69, 604–608, 924–928 and 963–968; data/bgo_ambient.toml, decay parameters; src/frontend/sipm.jl, branching_mean and n_crosstalk.

**What is wrong and evidence:** Two numerical conversions in the proposed photoelectron budget are inconsistent with its inputs.

First, the Citiroc fraction of 0.29 describes a pure 300 ns decay. For the configured mixture of 90% at 300 ns and 10% at 60 ns, the same noiseless voltage-preamp calculation gives 0.32557 at 87.5 ns shaping. The cold fraction is 0.12316. Multiplying by the respective yields gives a cold/warm stored-light ratio of 13,500 × 0.12316 / (8,000 × 0.32557) = 0.6384: approximately 36% less, rather than the quoted 28% less. This is the continuous mean-light calculation underlying the document's fraction, not a replacement for the full stochastic peak-detector simulation.

Second, the budget says to divide by 1/(1−p) for branching crosstalk. The implemented Borel model has mean 1/[1−lambda], with lambda = −ln(1−p). At p = 0.195 these are approximately 1.242 and 1.277. Using the stated budget formula would overestimate recovered primary photoelectrons by about 2.8%, before the afterpulse correction. The generator itself uses the correct branching process.

**Consequence:** The decision to omit cold Citiroc remains supported by this particular stored-light comparison, but its numerical rationale and a future reconstruction of primary photoelectrons would be wrong. These errors also complicate comparison with the digitiser's separately biased charge calibration.

**Proposed fix:** Compute the stated fractions from the actual material mixtures and use the same branching mean in the inverse budget as in the generator. Keep the distinction between a deterministic shaped-light fraction and the stochastic, pedestal-subtracted recorded fraction. Evidence: basic_checks.jl/basic_checks.log and final_calculations.jl.

### 6. The resolution decomposition reports unresolved residuals as physical contributions

**Where:** fullsim_analysis/scripts/room_temperature_study.jl, lines 49–50, 99 and 116–119; room_temperature_study.tex, Resolution and Comparison sections; bgo_250k.csv, term_readout column.

**What is wrong and evidence:** Differences of squared percentile widths are still truncated to zero and reported without uncertainties. I recomputed this statistic from the current saved arrays and made 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples per case. For the cold digitiser, 50.6% of resamples give a negative squared readout residual; after the code's truncation, its 95% interval is 0–2.53% FWHM-equivalent. The table's zero therefore does not establish a negligible readout contribution. For cold Radioroc the corresponding interval is 0–2.77%, with 23.7% negative before truncation. At room temperature the CsI(Tl) digitiser and Radioroc readout intervals are 0–3.01% and 0.27–3.36%, respectively. Their quoted 1.6% and 2.6% do not establish an ordering.

These intervals describe the uncertainty of the existing residual statistic; they do not validate it as an additive physical variance. Squared 16–84% widths do not in general add under convolution, and these subtractions do not separately identify shaping, dark pulses and electronics noise.

The unusual BGO yield comparison does not isolate a peak-fitter failure. In the historical and current saved Radioroc photopeak arrays, the robust recorded-energy width changes from 9.300% to 8.704%. A paired bootstrap gives a change of −0.595 percentage points with a standard deviation of 0.299 points. Thus a substantial change also exists outside the Gaussian spectral fit. For the current full spectrum, refitting both calibration peaks and the energy peak in 160 bootstrap samples gives a resolution standard deviation of 0.202 points, versus the script's 0.164-point approximation. For the cold digitiser the values are 0.227 versus 0.142 points. This check includes sampling variation in the energy calibration; it is not a measurement of all systematic errors.

**Consequence:** The smaller readout terms and their relative ranking are not determined by these samples. The 9.15% to 8.33% change should not be attributed quantitatively to the 5% light increase alone. The comparison needs more statistics and uncertainty propagation, but I found no evidence here that warrants labelling the entire change a code defect.

**Proposed fix:** Report intervals or upper limits for unresolved components, retain signed squared residuals in diagnostic output, and propagate the complete fit/calibration procedure when comparing resolutions. Use controlled replays with individual effects changed to support causal attributions. Evidence: analysis_checks.jl/analysis_checks.log; final_calculations.jl/final_calculations.log; the historical array was extracted into old_room_temperature.h5 from the parent of commit 9c500e6.

### 7. A dark-rate law consistent with the quoted cold measurement changes the 250 K threshold

**Where:** src/frontend/sipm.jl, lines 127–135; data/sipm_s14160.toml, dark_doubling_k; fullsim_design.tex, lines 449–454 and parameter table; fullsim_runs/2026-10_bgo250k/frontend_bgo_250k_digitizer_g4.toml, threshold_counts.

**What is uncertain and evidence:** The factor-two-per-9-K law predicts 2.482 kHz at 196 K and 6.5 V, substantially below the document's quoted 8–15 kHz measurement. The existing test only requires the prediction to be below 20 kHz, so it does not test agreement with that range. This is a numerical inconsistency with the stated anchor; it does not establish which temperature law or measurement interpretation is correct.

As a sensitivity calculation, I retained the warm reference and the existing PDE scaling, and used log-linear temperature laws passing through 10 or 15 kHz at 196 K. They predict 306.4 or 370.9 kHz per channel at 250 K and 5 V, compared with the current 158.9 kHz. Each was tested on 5,000 empty 8 µs digitiser records, retaining the existing gain distribution, Borel crosstalk, afterpulses, pulse and noise. The counts and one-sided Poisson upper limits use the same low-occupancy rate approximation as the scan:

| Dark rate at 250 K | Threshold | Triggers / 5,000 | Rate | 95% upper limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current 158.9 kHz | 320 counts | 9 | 0.225 kHz | 0.393 kHz |
| 306.4 kHz alternative | 320 counts | 37 | 0.925 kHz | 1.217 kHz |
| 306.4 kHz alternative | 384 counts | 3 | 0.075 kHz | 0.194 kHz |
| 370.9 kHz alternative | 320 counts | 62 | 1.550 kHz | 1.915 kHz |
| 370.9 kHz alternative | 384 counts | 15 | 0.375 kHz | 0.577 kHz |

**Consequence:** The proposed 320-count threshold passes the criterion under the current rate but fails it in both alternative tests; 384 counts passes. Signal acceptance under the alternative rates was not recalculated, so this is not a completed replacement operating point. The alternative temperature laws are sensitivity models, not validated extrapolations.

The sample size of 1,500 is sufficient to establish a sub-kHz bound when no events are seen over the configured cold digitiser exposure: its upper limit is 0.250 kHz. It is much less decisive for the short Citiroc record, where even zero counts gives 0.908 kHz. The acknowledged BGO Citiroc exception should therefore remain an exception, not be described as meeting the rule.

**Proposed fix:** Reconcile the dark-rate anchors and test the threshold against the resulting rate range. Preserve the distinction between a measured rate below 1 kHz and an upper confidence bound below 1 kHz. Evidence: dark_sensitivity.jl/dark_sensitivity.log, analytic.log.

### 8. The Radioroc datasheet does provide a signal-input termination option, which changes the calibration timing

**Where:** fullsim_design.tex, lines 480–483 and 745–748; papers/radioroc2-datasheet.txt, lines 1504–1513; src/frontend/sipm.jl, physical_pulse and with_input.

**What is wrong or uncertain and evidence:** The design says the 100-ohm option belongs to the bias DAC and therefore does not specify the signal-input impedance. The datasheet explicitly says that one DAC provides 100 ohms at the ASIC input so that the SiPM can be connected without an external resistor. For the high-impedance DAC option it calls for an external resistor with a 100 nF capacitor to ground. The DAC's bias function does not make its termination irrelevant to the signal.

This does not prove that 50 ohms is wrong for the eventual board: an external 50-ohm termination is a possible choice. It does mean the available 100-ohm mode should not be dismissed. Within the project's own circuit model, changing 50 to 100 ohms moves the single-cell peak from 69.3 to 92.4 ns and doubles its long time constant to 200 ns. For the 100 ns Radioroc high-gain calibration, the noise-free crossing-to-maximum hold changes from 229 to 268 ns, and the removed room-temperature dark baseline from 1.474 to 2.010 cell equivalents. These changes affect the multiplicity-assignment problem in finding 3. The cold 1.8 µs shaped-light fraction changes only from 0.8428 to 0.8458, so this calculation does not establish a large change in the cold energy response.

**Consequence:** The calibration hold and the dark-spectrum structure depend on which documented termination mode is chosen. The present pulse is also not demonstrably the slowest plausible case, as the note calls it.

**Proposed fix:** Describe the two termination arrangements correctly, make the selected arrangement part of the configuration, and compare calibration settings under both until the board circuit is established. The actual termination and complete preamplifier transfer remain unverified. Evidence: basic_checks.log and final_calculations.log.

### 9. The local-channel Citiroc calibration idealisation changes the fitted gain under a common hold

**Where:** src/frontend/citiroc.jl, held_at_crossings and hg_at_crossings; fullsim_analysis/scripts/calibration.jl, lines 50–78; room_temperature_study.tex, Calibration section; the A5202 acquisition distinction specified in REVIEW_BRIEF.md.

**What is uncertain and evidence:** The calibration allows every channel to acquire after its own crossing, with only that channel's hold interval making it busy. A common OR trigger and common hold select a different subset of pulses. I compared both selections on the same 1,600 simulated 64-channel BGO dark records, using the production 25 ns high-gain shaping and its 31 ns crossing-to-maximum delay.

For the common-hold diagnostic, all channel crossings were merged chronologically; the earliest available crossing started a common busy interval until its hold, and the initiating channel supplied the calibration value. No ADC/readout dead time was added, to isolate the common-hold effect. Pooling amplitudes after dividing out the known simulated channel factors, the existing ladder finder returned 9.341 counts per cell for local acquisition and 9.039 for common acquisition, a 3.2% shift. The accepted entries were approximately 1.205 million and 144,643 respectively. This is a controlled selection comparison, not a complete A5202 acquisition simulation or a demonstration of the per-channel scatter on that board.

**Consequence:** The local acquisition's fitted/true gain and calibration statistics cannot be transferred unchanged to a common-hold board. The observed shift is material compared with the quoted few-percent calibration scatter, and would propagate to the recorded photoelectron scale if left uncorrected. It does not establish that common-hold calibration is impossible.

**Proposed fix:** Add an acquisition model with the actual global trigger, hold, channel selection and readout busy time, and repeat the per-channel ladder study through that model. Until then, state the local-channel results as an idealised calibration prediction. Evidence: common_hold.jl/common_hold.log.
