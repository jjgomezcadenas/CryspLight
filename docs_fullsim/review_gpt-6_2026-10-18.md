## Verified findings

### 1. Radioroc2 starts its long shaper without the preceding dark current

- **Where:** src/frontend/shaping.jl, lines 25–41; src/frontend/radioroc.jl, line 43 and chain construction; ambient_study.tex, Pedestal and BGO/Radioroc2 sections, especially lines 356–373.
- **Claim:** The external-trigger pedestal represents a random ambient acquisition; a charge threshold of 40 accepts only 72% of BGO deposits between 200 and 300 keV, and the residual energy-scale offset is 64 keV.
- **Problem:** The waveform starts at −200 ns and every integrator starts at zero. Earlier avalanches are discarded by bin_train!, even when the dark-generation window contains them. A 1.8 µs shaper therefore has almost no dark history at the decay. External triggering at zero does not make this a stationary random-trigger pedestal. In a matched 2,000-record, single-channel test, retaining history from −16 µs changed the pedestal from 12.154 ± 0.078 to 18.172 ± 0.076 photoelectron equivalents at 3 V, and from 9.125 ± 0.065 to 13.683 ± 0.064 at 2 V. These are increases of 385 and 292 equivalents over 64 channels. More directly, replaying the same 500 existing BGO deposits in the 200–300 keV range with the production operating parameters changed acceptance from 76.0% with the short history to 98.6% with the long history. Both versions accepted all 500 matched photopeak events. Their mean photopeak trigger times changed from 1,089 to 785 ns. The 76% is the matched subsample result, not a replacement estimate for the published 72%. These tests establish a substantial effect on the threshold conclusion. They do not establish a corrected full-spectrum resolution. Warm the filter before the observation window, then recompute pedestal, trigger efficiency, energy scale and resolution together. Evidence: checks.jl/checks.log and history_check.jl/history_check.log.
- **Severity:** changes a bench setting; changes numbers and conclusions in the documents.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 2. The digitiser's stored charge is not the integral of its simulated waveform

- **Where:** src/frontend/digitizer.jl, lines 18, 72 and 123–138; data/sipm_s14160.toml, lines 32–33; ambient_study.tex, digitiser calibration and integration descriptions.
- **Claim:** The calculation without stored waveforms reproduces gated, baseline-subtracted integration of the simulated pulses.
- **Problem:** The waveform shape is fixed by the SiPM rise and fall constants, but its integral is independently set to peak × 4.4 samples. Those definitions disagree. For an isolated avalanche at 5 V and gain 4, with noise disabled, external trigger zero and the BGO gate, the stored model charge is 281.600 count-samples; summing the simulated waveform at the 8 ns sampling interval gives 542.660. The actual area/peak ratio is 8.479 samples, a factor 1.927 above 4.4. Thus peak calibration, the trigger waveform and integrated-charge calibration cannot all describe the same pulse. In particular, the white-noise charge in count-samples is converted into photoelectron units with the inconsistent area. A common rescaling of signal and calibration would cancel in the energy scale, so this is not a claim that the entire energy resolution changes by 1.927. The electronic-noise contribution relative to signal does change. Derive the integration scale from the adopted pulse, or adopt a pulse consistent with the measured area/peak ratio and repeat the relevant predictions. Evidence: the first calculation in checks.jl/checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes numbers and conclusions in the documents, including the charge calibration and noise prediction.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 3. Dark-trigger exposure and the stated zero-event bound are incorrect

- **Where:** fullsim_analysis/scripts/trigger_scan.jl, lines 19 and 55–58; ambient_study.tex, lines 296–298 and 330–333.
- **Claim:** The BGO Citiroc dark-trigger rate is about 0.3 kHz; 1,500 empty BGO digitiser records bound its rate below 0.3 kHz.
- **Problem:** The denominator is the dark-generation interval, including time outside the waveform on which triggering is tested. For BGO Citiroc the two observed triggers are divided by 1,500 × 4.5 µs, giving 0.296 kHz, although the waveform spans only 2.2 µs: even before correcting start-up effects, the corresponding value is 0.606 kHz. For CsI(Tl) Citiroc, seven triggers give 0.753 kHz using 6.2 µs rather than the reported 0.622 kHz using 7.5 µs. The digitiser's observable interval is 5 µs, not 5.5 µs. With zero triggers in 1,500 records its one-sided 95% Poisson upper limit is −ln(0.05)/(1,500 × 5 µs) = 0.399 kHz. Even the longer denominator gives 0.363 kHz. The note supplies no confidence level for its smaller bound. Count live observation time after warming the response, state a confidence level, and distinguish a zero count from a measured zero rate. The event implementation also returns at most one trigger, so fraction/window is only a low-occupancy approximation to a rate. Evidence: stored trigger_scan.csv, configuration intervals and tails.py/tails.log.
- **Severity:** changes numbers and conclusions in the documents.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 4. An earlier isolated crossing suppresses a later valid majority trigger

- **Where:** src/frontend/digitizer.jl, lines 99–118; analogous first-crossing selection in src/frontend/citiroc.jl, lines 128–138.
- **Claim:** Majority two triggers when two channels cross threshold within the coincidence interval.
- **Problem:** Only the first crossing of each channel is retained. A channel that crosses alone early in the record cannot contribute to a later coincidence. A noiseless two-channel reproduction at gain 4 and threshold 480 counts has an 8-cell dark pulse in channel 1 at −1,000 ns, followed by simultaneous 8-cell pulses in channels 1 and 2 at zero. The current code returns triggered=false, although the later pair exceeds threshold together after the first pulse has decayed. This is an implicit record-long channel veto, not the stated coincidence rule. Track subsequent crossings and evaluate coincidences chronologically, with any intended dead time specified explicitly. The aggregate efficiency bias in the production sample has not been measured here. Evidence: the coincidence check in checks.jl/checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes the trigger model and potentially its proposed setting and efficiency conclusions.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 5. The Citiroc calibration hold is early, not late

- **Where:** fullsim_analysis/scripts/calibration.jl, lines 40–41; ambient_study.tex, lines 300–302; src/frontend/shaping.jl, lines 125–129.
- **Claim:** The fitted gain of 0.885 of the true value occurs because holding one nominal peaking time after the discriminator falls slightly after the maximum.
- **Problem:** With exactly those calibration settings, an isolated noiseless avalanche at zero triggers at 20 ns. The hold is at 107.5 ns, but the slow waveform reaches its maximum at 142 ns. Its held amplitude is 0.889933 of the maximum, reproducing the reported deficit without dark pile-up. Convolving the finite device pulse with the shaper moves the maximum beyond the nominal shaper time. Within this implementation, reaching the maximum requires about 122 ns after the trigger, an increase of about 34.5 ns in the hold delay. The explanation has the sign wrong and would suggest the wrong direction of adjustment. This is an internal-model timing result, not a measurement of the chip's optimal hold. Evidence: checks.jl/checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes a bench calibration setting and the explanation in the document.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 6. The 3.4% Citiroc saturation flag does not establish the claimed spectral distortion

- **Where:** src/frontend/citiroc.jl, lines 149–158; fullsim_analysis/lib/io.jl, line 21; ambient_study.tex, lines 404–406.
- **Claim:** Saturation in 3.4% of CsI(Tl) photopeak events bends the response, producing the peak-position ratio of 2.44.
- **Problem:** The input-saturation flag uses the sum of all avalanche charge in the event, including avalanches after the hold. It then clips the held amplitude only if that amplitude exceeds the same ceiling. Those are different quantities for a long scintillation pulse. In the saved 7,139 photopeak events, 241 have the saturation flag, but the largest stored channel amplitude is only 391.469 equivalents, below the approximately 994-equivalent input ceiling at 2.7 V. The input clamp therefore changes none of these photopeak amplitudes. One photopeak event reaches the high-gain ADC ceiling, but the analysis uses charge_pe, saved before ADC clipping, rather than that ADC value. The reported flag fraction consequently does not demonstrate that the simulated 511 keV response was compressed by saturation. Removing or varying the actual nonlinear response is needed to attribute the 2.44 ratio to saturation; a flag alone cannot do so. Evidence: production events.h5 and the ADC comparison in checks.jl/checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes the conclusion about saturation and the evidence supporting the chosen Citiroc overvoltage.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 7. The physics spectra do not exercise the dark-derived calibration described in the note

- **Where:** fullsim_analysis/lib/io.jl, lines 21 and 31–35; fullsim_analysis/scripts/ambient_study.jl, energy-scale construction and line 101; ambient_study.tex, Calibration and light-chain descriptions.
- **Claim:** The procedure calibrates channels with dark-pulse peaks, subtracts the pedestal, and obtains the reported photoelectron-equivalent charges and spectra.
- **Problem:** The gain fits produced by calibration.jl are not applied to these spectra. The main q array comes from events/charge_pe, which for Citiroc is a simulation diagnostic before ADC quantisation and clipping. The Radioroc low-gain alternative is converted using the supplied /calib gains, not the fitted dark gains. Consequently the study predicts performance with the generator's calibration rather than demonstrating the stated measurement procedure. This matters at the quoted precision: applying the common Citiroc fitted/true gain ratio of about 0.885 without a hold correction would increase the reported photoelectron-equivalent values by about 13%, from 726 to roughly 820 for BGO and from 1,461 to roughly 1,651 for CsI(Tl). A two-point energy calibration absorbs a common multiplicative factor, so this does not imply a 13% change in the keV resolution. Indeed, my separate reconstruction using digitised Citiroc amplitudes and the supplied gains changes the fitted resolutions by less than their reported statistical errors. The consequential discrepancy here is the light-chain units and the untested transfer from fitted dark gains to physics data. Either identify the results as predictions with known gains, or run the stated calibration through the digitised observables and propagate its bias and spread. Evidence: read_events, saved calibration outputs and checks.jl/checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes numbers and conclusions in the documents about calibration and recorded photoelectrons.
- **Confidence:** verified.

### 8. Small resolution components are unresolved, and the subtraction does not identify their physical cause

- **Where:** fullsim_analysis/scripts/ambient_study.jl, lines 87 and 104–107; ambient_study.tex, resolution-decomposition method and comparisons, including line 444.
- **Claim:** Differences in quadrature of robust widths separate crosstalk/afterpulsing and readout contributions, with the CsI(Tl) Radioroc readout term identified as the smallest, at 1.8%.
- **Problem:** Only 3,000 replayed events determine each component, no component uncertainty is propagated, and negative squared differences are silently replaced by zero. A paired bootstrap of the saved photoelectron, fired-cell and recorded-energy arrays, with 1,000 resamples, gives negative SiPM squared terms in 42%, 37% and 65% of resamples for CsI(Tl) Citiroc, digitiser and Radioroc respectively. The corresponding 95% intervals after the code's truncation are 0–1.19%, 0–1.21% and 0–0.94%. These small terms are not resolved. The analogous CsI(Tl) readout intervals are 2.11–3.05% for the digitiser and 0.57–2.41% for Radioroc; the stated ordering needs an uncertainty on their difference. These are bootstrap intervals for the existing statistic, not a validation of it as a physical variance decomposition. Squared 16–84% widths have no general additive-variance identity, and subtracting them does not isolate shaping, electronics noise and dark pile-up. Controlled replays switching those effects separately are needed for the specific causal statements. The larger Citiroc readout contribution is much better supported than the small-component precision. Evidence: decomposition.jl/decomposition.log and the saved study.h5 arrays.
- **Severity:** changes numbers and conclusions in the documents about resolution components and their origin.
- **Confidence:** verified for the statistical instability and what the implemented analysis measures; the physical allocation remains unestablished.

### 9. Event reseeding does not eliminate thread-dependent ASIC noise

- **Where:** src/frontend/shaping.jl, lines 153–160; src/frontend/frontend_run.jl, lines 111–114 and 135–152; fullsim_analysis/lib/emulate.jl, lines 39–45.
- **Claim:** Per-event reseeding makes the threaded front-end response reproducible independently of event scheduling.
- **Problem:** Each thread constructs its own noise_generator with a different initial seed. The constructor estimates a noise normalisation from a finite random trace and retains that value in a closure. Reseeding the event's RNG does not change this cached normalisation. Constructing two otherwise identical Citiroc chains with seeds 1 and 2, then reseeding both identically for each of 1,000 empty external-trigger events, gives different digitised high-gain values in 136 events. This is not merely a sub-rounding floating-point discrepancy. Radioroc uses the same mechanism. The noise scale must be deterministic and common across equivalent chains if the claimed scheduling independence is required. The passing frozen checks do not exercise this property. Evidence: reseed_check.jl/reseed_check.log; the isolated stored-amplitude comparison is also in checks.log.
- **Severity:** changes a reproducibility conclusion and the required test coverage.
- **Confidence:** verified.

## Findings whose physical alternative remains unverified

### 12. A plausible crosstalk multiplicity model changes the recommended digitiser threshold

- **Where:** src/frontend/sipm.jl, lines 165–170; ambient_study.tex, digitiser trigger proposal at 480 counts and discussion of crosstalk companions.
- **Claim:** A geometric distribution of simultaneous companions adequately supports the proposed majority-two threshold of 7.5 photoelectrons at 5 V.
- **Problem:** Fixing the probability of any crosstalk does not fix the high-multiplicity tail that drives this threshold. Poisson branching, producing a Borel total-cluster distribution, is a physically motivated alternative discussed and compared with geometric models in [Vinogradov, Analytical models of probability distribution and excess noise factor of Solid State Photomultiplier signals with crosstalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2014). I set its branching mean to −ln(1−0.195), preserving the model's 19.5% probability of at least one companion, and kept the primary dark rate, afterpulsing, pulse shape, gain and noise assumptions. In 10,000 empty BGO digitiser records per model, the geometric model produced three triggers at 480 counts; the branching model produced 232. With the actual 5 µs observation interval these correspond to approximately 0.06 and 4.64 kHz. Raising the branching-model threshold to 576 counts, nine photoelectrons, produced 17 triggers, approximately 0.34 kHz. Thus a plausible multiplicity alternative moves the proposed threshold needed to remain below 1 kHz. The signal acceptance at 576 counts was not established by this check. These numbers use the current event-trigger implementation, including the limitation in finding 4. They are a sensitivity result, not evidence that the Borel model is the correct model of this device. A measured multiplicity tail, rather than just the integrated crosstalk probability, is needed to settle the setting. Evidence: crosstalk_check.jl/crosstalk_check.log.
- **Severity:** changes a proposed bench threshold under a quantified plausible alternative.
- **Confidence:** plausible physical alternative; the simulated sensitivity is verified.

### 13. The Citiroc preamplifier assumption materially affects its pedestal prediction

- **Where:** src/frontend/shaping.jl, lines 97–107; papers/citiroc1a-datasheet.txt, section 6.1, lines 593 onward, and section 6.2; ambient_study.tex, Citiroc pedestal and recorded-fraction discussion.
- **Claim:** The preamplifier integrates the SiPM current, so CR–RC² can be replaced by three low-pass stages applied to the device pulse.
- **Problem:** That cancellation is justified for an ideal charge integrator, whereas the Citiroc datasheet explicitly describes voltage-sensitive preamplifiers with voltage gain Cin/Cf, followed by CRRC² slow shapers. The text alone does not establish the complete SiPM-to-input transfer function, so it is insufficient to prove the current implementation wrong. It does, however, motivate checking a voltage response followed by an uncancelled CR–RC² stage. In a scratch comparison I normalised both models to a unit single-avalanche maximum and adjusted the alternative time constant to match the existing pulse's approximately 142 ns maximum. For the stated BGO and CsI(Tl) decay mixtures, the maximum response per emitted primary changed from 0.4206 to 0.4395 and from 0.1524 to 0.1564. More consequentially, in 2,000 stationary dark records at 3 V, with a 300 ns peak window and electronics noise disabled, the mean pedestal changed from 1.6665 to 0.8248 equivalents per channel, or from 106.7 to 52.8 over 64 channels. These are controlled model comparisons, not replacements for the published full-chain numbers. The alternative is not a validated hardware transfer function, but its factor-two pedestal effect makes the integration assumption consequential. Establish the input transfer from circuit information or a measured single-avalanche waveform before interpreting the pedestal and recorded fraction quantitatively. Evidence: shaping_check.jl/shaping_check.log.
- **Severity:** changes predicted pedestal and recorded-fraction numbers under a quantified plausible alternative.
- **Confidence:** plausible; the actual Citiroc transfer function remains unresolved by the available text.
