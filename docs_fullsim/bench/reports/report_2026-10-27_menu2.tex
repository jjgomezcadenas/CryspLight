\section{Goal}

\begin{itemize}
\item Above breakdown every channel produces dark pulses, several MHz per channel, almost all
  of one photoelectron (pe); those with crosstalk are of 2, 3, \ldots~pe. They are the test
  signal of this menu.
\item Menu 2 sets two parameters of the data taking with the crystal:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the threshold of the discriminator of each channel (TD\_CoarseThreshold, in DAC
    units), to trigger on one photoelectron and not on the noise;
  \item the hold delay (HoldDelay), so that the window of the peak detector contains the
    maximum of the shaped pulse.
  \end{itemize}
\item Two measurements:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{staircase}: trigger rate of each channel against its threshold; gives the
    edge of the noise and the size of one photoelectron in DAC units;
  \item the \emph{hold-delay scan}: stored value of a channel against the hold delay; gives
    the response of the peak detector to one avalanche, hence the minimum hold delay, and the
    gain of the channel at this bias.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}

\begin{itemize}
\item Settings common to all scans: HV\_Vbias 47.2~V (43~V at the SiPM); TD\_CoarseThreshold
  185; preamplifier gains 55; peak-stretch mode; low gain at 87.5~ns.
\item Staircase (scan 100): 64 channels, thresholds 150 to 300 in steps of 1, 0.5~s per

\item Hold-delay scans (Table~\ref{tab:scans}): trigger limited to one channel (the shooting

\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Staircase}

What the DAQ does:
\begin{itemize}
\item sets the threshold $\theta$ of the 64 discriminators, counts the triggers of each
  channel for 0.5~s, lowers $\theta$ by one unit, and repeats from 300 to 150;
\item the result is the trigger rate $R_i(\theta)$ of each channel $i$.
\end{itemize}
What it shows:
\begin{itemize}
\item below the edge of the noise the discriminator fires on the noise of the fast shaper and
  the rate saturates: the plateau;
\item above the edge the rate falls in steps, one per photoelectron, because a dark pulse of
  $n$~pe triggers only while $\theta$ is below its amplitude; the distance between steps is
  one photoelectron in DAC units.
\end{itemize}
Definitions:
\begin{align*}
R_i^\mathrm{max} &= \max_\theta R_i(\theta), \qquad
\theta_i^\mathrm{e} = \max\{\theta : R_i(\theta) \ge R_i^\mathrm{max}/2\}, \\
\Delta\theta &= \text{distance between consecutive steps of } R_i(\theta),
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item $R_i^\mathrm{max}$: the plateau;
\item $\theta_i^\mathrm{e}$: the edge of the plateau, the highest threshold at which the
  channel still triggers on the noise;
\item $\Delta\theta$: one photoelectron in DAC units.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hold-delay scan}

What the chip does (peak-stretch mode):
\begin{itemize}
\item a dark pulse crosses the threshold of the fast shaper at $t = 0$ and triggers;
\item the board opens the window of the peak detector after its own latency and closes it
  with the hold, a time HoldDelay later;
\item the chip stores the maximum of the slow shaper inside the window.
\end{itemize}
What the DAQ does:
\begin{itemize}
\item limits the trigger to one channel, the shooting channel: every event is one of its dark
  pulses;
\item for each delay $\tau$ collects 10\,000 events and stores the spectrum $S_i(v;\tau)$ of
  every channel $i$ (bins of 16 counts);
\item repeats for $\tau$ from 0 to 248 or 496~ns.
\end{itemize}
What the spectrum of the shooting channel shows:
\begin{itemize}
\item a ladder of peaks at 1, 2, 3~pe: the dark pulse that triggered and its crosstalk;
\item if the hold arrives before the maximum of the shaped pulse, the stored value lies on
  its rising edge and the 1-pe peak sits below the full amplitude; once the window contains the
  maximum, the peak sits at the full amplitude and stays there;
\item the position of the 1-pe peak against $\tau$ is therefore the response of the peak
  detector to one avalanche; its knee is the minimum hold delay;
\item the mean of the spectrum is not used for the knee: it also counts the dark pulses and
  the crosstalk that fall inside the window, and keeps growing with $\tau$.
\end{itemize}
Definitions, with $p_i$ the pedestal of the channel below breakdown at the same shaping:
\begin{align*}
q_1(\tau) &= \text{position of the 1-pe peak of } S_i(v;\tau), \\
G &= \operatorname{median}_{\text{last 8 points}} q_1(\tau) - p_i, \qquad
\tau_\mathrm{min} = \min\{\tau : q_1(\tau) - p_i \ge 0.95\,G\},
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item $q_1(\tau)$: the centre of the most populated bin between $p_i + 48$ and $p_i + 400$
  (the 1-pe region), interpolated with its two neighbouring bins;
\item $G$: the plateau of the 1-pe peak above the pedestal, the single-photoelectron gain of
  the channel at this bias;
\item $\tau_\mathrm{min}$: the knee of the response, the minimum hold delay;
\item stale points: the first points of a scan, at which the median over the non-shooting
  channels of the mean stored value has not yet dropped to its minimum over the scan;
\item model of the front end: the shaped pulse reaches its maximum 31~ns (25~ns of shaping)
  and 58~ns (50~ns) after the crossing of the threshold; the knee is expected there, shifted
  by the latency of the board.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary of results}

Main findings:
\begin{itemize}
\item The discriminator triggers on the noise below 179 to 182 DAC units and one photoelectron
  is 20 units: a threshold of 185 is a clean single-photoelectron trigger.
\item The peak detector reaches the maximum of a 25~ns pulse 32 to 40~ns after the trigger,
  where the model of the front end puts it; at 50~ns it takes 130 to 160~ns, 70 to 100~ns more
  than the model.
\item Once the window contains the maximum, the stored amplitude of one avalanche stays
  constant up to the longest delay scanned.
\item The 1-pe peak, not the mean, is the observable of the scan: the mean keeps growing with
  the dark pulses that the window collects.
\item The four channels scanned have gains between 150 and 182 counts per photoelectron at
  25~ns, 185 to 192 at 50~ns.
\end{itemize}
