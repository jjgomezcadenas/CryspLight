\noindent On 27 October 2026 the hold-delay programme of the Citiroc~1A front end (menu~2) was
run on board~1, with its SiPM matrix at 5~V of overvoltage: one threshold scan (staircase) and
six hold-delay scans at 25 and 50~ns of shaping. This report gives the threshold of the
discriminator, the response of the peak detector to a single dark pulse, the resulting minimum
hold delay at each shaping, and the single-photoelectron gain at 5~V of the four channels
scanned.

\section{Data}

The SiPMs were biased at 43~V (5~V above breakdown at the temperature of the bench), the
discriminator threshold at 185 DAC units in all scans, the preamplifier gains at 55 and the peak
detectors in peak-stretch mode. The staircase (scan 100) counts the triggers of each of the 64
channels at thresholds from 150 to 300, one unit at a time, 0.5~s per point. Each hold-delay scan
(Table~\ref{tab:scans}) limits the trigger to one channel, the shooting channel, and stores the
high-gain spectrum of every channel at each delay, with 10\,000 events per delay.

The DAQ applied each new delay with a lag of up to five points: at the start of every scan but
the first, all channels keep the values of the last delay of the previous scan. Those points are
recognised by the median of the non-shooting channels, which stays at the previous level and
then drops to the pedestal, and are left out of the analysis. The rise of the response at 50~ns
is therefore sampled from 80~ns on.

\section{Threshold}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{staircase_100.png}
\caption{Scan 100, 25~ns, 5~V of overvoltage: trigger rate of each channel of board 1 against
the threshold of its discriminator, and the rate of the OR of the 64 channels.}
\label{fig:staircase}
\end{figure}

Below a threshold of 179 to 182 units every channel triggers on the noise of its fast shaper
(Figure~\ref{fig:staircase}). Above it the rate falls in steps, one per photoelectron, with a
step of 20 DAC units per photoelectron; 30 units above the edge the channels count their dark
pulses at a median of $4.4\times 10^{5}$~Hz. Channels 61 and 63, which do not see their SiPM,
fall from the noise plateau to a few hertz within 10 units. The threshold of 185 used in the
hold-delay scans is 4 to 6 units above the edge, below one photoelectron.

In peak-stretch mode the chip stores the maximum of the slow shaper inside the window that opens
after the trigger and closes with the hold. The spectrum of the shooting channel
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}) has its 1-pe peak at 265~counts for a delay of 0 and at 315 from
32~ns on, with the 2-pe and 3-pe peaks at 505 and 700; the peaks are 190~counts apart. The
position of the 1-pe peak against the delay (Figure~\ref{fig:response}, right) is the response
of the peak detector to a single avalanche: at 25~ns it rises from the pedestal region to its
plateau at 32 to 40~ns in the four channels scanned and stays flat up to 248~ns; at 50~ns it
reaches its plateau at 130 to 160~ns (Table~\ref{tab:knees}). The mean of the stored value
(Figure~\ref{fig:response}, left) rises with the peak and keeps growing after it, as the window
collects more dark pulses and their crosstalk; the knee is read on the peak, not on the mean.

At 25~ns the knee coincides with the maximum of the shaped pulse in the model of the front end,
31~ns after the crossing of the threshold; at 50~ns the knee comes 70 to 100~ns after the
model's 58~ns.

The plateau of the 1-pe peak above the pedestal is the gain at 5~V: 175, 150, 159 and 182~counts
per photoelectron in channels 0, 20, 40 and 60 at 25~ns, and 185 to 192~counts in channel 0 at
50~ns.

\section{A window at a random time}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{hold_scan_window.png}
\caption{Mean of channel 32, which does not trigger, above its pedestal, against the delay of
the six scans.}
\label{fig:window}
\end{figure}

The channels that do not trigger see a window at a random time with respect to their own dark
pulses. Their mean grows linearly with the length of the window (Figure~\ref{fig:window}),
by 0.52~counts per nanosecond at 25~ns and 0.42 at 50~ns, that is 3.0 and 2.2 photoelectrons
per microsecond at the gain measured above. Their spectrum (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}, right)
is the pedestal with a ladder of 1, 2 and 3~pe that fills as the window lengthens.

\section{Values}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Threshold: the edge of the noise at 179 to 182 DAC units at 5~V and 25~ns; one
  photoelectron is 20 units; the threshold of 185 is below one photoelectron.
\item Minimum hold delay: 40~ns at 25~ns of shaping, 160~ns at 50~ns.
\item Single-photoelectron gain at 5~V, high gain, preamplifier gain 55: 150 to 182~counts per
  photoelectron at 25~ns in the four channels scanned, 185 to 192 at 50~ns.
\item In a window at a random time, a channel at 5~V accumulates 3.0 photoelectrons per
  microsecond at 25~ns and 2.2 at 50~ns.
\end{enumerate}
