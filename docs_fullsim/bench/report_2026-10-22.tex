Data measured by Laura Navarro on 22 October 2026 with five A5202 boards and the PET CAEN DAQ,
copied from \path{hyperion:/data/lnavarro/26_10_caen_measurements_jj}. The runs repeat bench
note 1 (Citiroc noise) after JMB solved the saturation of 21 October. Boards are numbered as in
the DAQ (board 0--4 = chain,node 0,0 / 0,1 / 1,0 / 1,1 / 1,2), channels 0--63; the two Citirocs
of a board are channels 0--31 and 32--63. The problems found so far, and their status, are
kept in a findings log (F1--F10), cited below.

\noindent Each run has 10\,012--10\,016 periodic triggers (1~ms) read by the five boards, with
no trigger lost. The low gain is at 87.5~ns in all runs. \texttt{HV\_Vbias} = 40.7~V with
\texttt{HV\_IndivAdj} = 0, so the SiPMs see 36.5~V, below breakdown (F2); the HV current is
0.2--0.35~$\mu$A. The detector temperature is 24.0--25.0~$^\circ$C on all boards. The logbook
has one row per run and board and agrees with the files. The DAQ now records
\texttt{PeakDetectorMode} (PEAK\_STRETCH, applied on all boards), which shows that it includes
the pull requests merged on 20 October.

\section{What works}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The saturation is gone} (F1). No value of any channel is at 8191.
\item \textbf{Pedestals.} The mean of the high gain is different in each channel, between 50
  and 310 ADC counts (Figure~\ref{fig:ped}), and the low gain between 55 and 280. Between
  run 26 and run 34, taken 23 minutes apart with the same settings, the pedestals move by
  $-0.4$ counts on average and by at most 1.6 counts: they are stable.
\item \textbf{Noise.} The rms of the high gain is 12--13 counts at all three shapings, and that
  of the low gain about 6 counts, the same on all boards. With a longer HoldDelay the
  pedestal rises and narrows, as expected for the peak stretch: at 496~ns it is 0.8 rms
  higher and 0.6 times as wide as at 0~ns. How these numbers compare with the noise model of
  the simulation is the subject of a separate note (model against data, noise); in short, the
  noise depends much less on the shaping than the model predicts, and 25--40\% of its variance
  is common to the 32 channels of a chip.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problems}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Half of boards 1 and 4 reads zero (F7).} Channels 0--31 of boards 1 and 4 give 0
  in every event, in both gains and all nine runs; channels 32--63 of the same boards are
  normal. The configuration stored in the files is the same for these two chips as for the
  others: readout, discriminators and preamplifiers enabled, the same gains, thresholds and
  shaping, and per-channel pedestal calibrations of 102--271 counts like those of the chips
  that work. \texttt{Pedestal} = 0 is not the cause either, since the live channels have
  essentially no value at zero. The same chip failing on two boards with a normal
  configuration points to the readout (firmware or DAQ), presumably software. 64 of the 320
  channels have therefore no data.
\item \textbf{Board 0 jumps as a whole (F10).} In 0.1--0.4\% of the events of runs 26--32, all
  the channels of board 0 move together by 60--230 counts, up or down, often in two
  consecutive triggers. This is what made board 0 look noisier in run 26: its rms is 15.9
  counts, but its robust width is 11.9, the same as on the other boards. Runs 33 and 34 have
  no such events, and the other boards have none (board 3, one event in three runs).
\item \textbf{Firmware (F9).} Board 2 runs firmware 0xa7070800, the others 0xa1230708; boards 3
  and 4 report acquisition state 16387 and service status 0x400d, the others 3 and 0x000d.
  No effect is visible in the data: board 2 reads normally.
\item \textbf{The channels without avalanches cannot be found from the pedestal (F4).} A SiPM
  at the input raises the noise of its preamplifier, so a channel with a bad contact should
  be quieter. Figure~\ref{fig:noise} shows the rms of each channel divided by the median of
  its board: at 87.5~ns the six channels of board 0 that were silent in the staircases sit at
  0.82--0.87, but channels with avalanches reach 0.81--0.89. The pedestal does not separate
  them, and the staircases of all five boards are needed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Questions and requests}

\textbf{For JMB.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Why do channels 0--31 of boards 1 and 4 read 0 (F7)? Would Janus, CAEN's software, read
  them on board 1?
\item Is anything different in the power, ground or cabling of board 0, which jumps as a whole
  in a few events per thousand (F10)?
\item What was the cause of the saturation of 21 October (F1)? Where does the
  \texttt{0xDEADBEEF} of scan 11 come from (F6)? Why does board 2 run a different firmware,
  and what do acquisition state 16387 and status 0x400d of boards 3 and 4 mean (F9)?
\item The manifests record DAQ revision 716d093, which is not on GitHub: is it a local
  build? The configuration decoded from the Citiroc register shows
  \texttt{PeakDetectorHigh} = false on all chips, while the audit gives PEAK\_STRETCH: is
  the bit active low?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{For Laura.} Menu 1 is complete, except for the 64 channels of F7, which will
need runs 26--34 repeated once JMB has solved it. The next session is menu 2 (bench note 2,
hold-delay scan), as written, with three corrections:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{HV\_Vbias} = 47.2~V. The note asks for about 43~V, but the board subtracts 4.2~V
  (F2), and 5~V of overvoltage needs about 43~V at the SiPM.
\item \texttt{FastShaperInput} = HG-PA, set explicitly. The DAQ now applies it, and the
  production configuration asks for LG-PA; the precaution of the note (staircase first, so
  that the fast shaper is left on the high gain) is no longer needed.
\item With five boards, the scan triggers on one channel of board 0 and the trigger masks of
  the other boards are set to zero. The staircase of step 1 is taken on all five boards: it
  will show which channels see their SiPM (F4, F5).
\end{enumerate}
