\begin{frame}{The session}
\begin{itemize}
\item Boards 1, 2 and 4 (A5202: two Citiroc~1A, a 64-channel SiPM matrix each), CAEN adapter
  boards, FPGA firmware 0xa7070800; read by the DAQ as boards 0, 1, 2.
\item Peak-stretch mode: at each trigger the peak detector follows the slow shaper from the
  opening of the window, holds the highest value reached and stops at the hold (HoldDelay
  after the trigger); the ADC reads that value.
\item Three menus:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pedestal noise}, SiPMs below breakdown: runs 91--99, three boards.
  \item \textbf{Threshold and hold delay}, SiPMs at 43~V: staircase 100, hold-delay scans
    101--106, board 1.
  \item \textbf{Single-photoelectron calibration}, periodic trigger at 43, 42, 41~V: runs
    108--112, three boards; staircases 113--115, board 1.

\section{Menu 1: pedestal noise}

\begin{frame}{Menu 1: dishes served, data}
\begin{columns}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Dishes served}
\begin{itemize}
\item Below breakdown there is no signal: the stored value is the pedestal, its fluctuation
  the noise of the electronics.
\item For every channel: pedestal and noise; dependence on the shaping and on the window;
  the part common to the chip (coherent) and the part proper to the channel (incoherent).
\end{itemize}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Data}
\begin{itemize}
\item Nine runs, 10\,000 events per board; SiPMs at 36.5~V; periodic trigger at 1~kHz.
\item Preamplifier gains 55; low gain at 87.5~ns.
\item Runs 91--93: 25, 50, 87.5~ns at HoldDelay 0.
\item Runs 94--98: 25~ns, HoldDelay 96--496~ns.
\item Run 99: repeats 91.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Menu 1: method}
Channel $i$, event $e$, high-gain value $v_i(e)$:
\begin{align*}
p_i &= \operatorname{median}_e v_i(e), \quad d_i(e) = v_i(e) - p_i, \quad
\mathrm{CM}(e) = \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i d_i(e), \quad d_i(e) = b_i\,\mathrm{CM}(e) + r_i(e),\\
\sigma_\mathrm{tot}^2 &= \langle d_i^2\rangle = \sigma_\mathrm{coh}^2 + \sigma_\mathrm{inc}^2,
\quad \sigma_\mathrm{coh}^2 = \langle b_i^2\,\mathrm{CM}^2\rangle, \quad
\sigma_\mathrm{inc}^2 = \langle r_i^2\rangle.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item $p_i$ pedestal, $d_i$ deviation, $\mathrm{CM}$ common mode of the $n$ channels of the
  chip, $b_i$ slope of the channel against it, $r_i$ incoherent part.
\item Set aside: jumps of the chip ($|\mathrm{CM}| > 6\,s_\mathrm{CM}$), outliers
  ($|d_i - \mathrm{CM}| > 5\,s_i$); $s$ the robust width.
\item Blind channel: no signal from its SiPM, takes no common mode ($b_i < 0.75$).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Menu 2: threshold and hold delay}

\begin{frame}{Menu 2: dishes served, data}
\begin{columns}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Dishes served}
\begin{itemize}
\item Above breakdown every channel produces dark pulses of 1~pe (2, 3 with crosstalk): the
  test signal.
\item Two parameters of the data taking: the threshold of the discriminator, to trigger on one
  photoelectron and not on the noise; the hold delay, so that the window contains the maximum
  of the shaped pulse.
\end{itemize}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Data}, board 1, 43~V, threshold 185
\begin{itemize}
\item Staircase 100: trigger rate of the 64 channels against the threshold, 150--300 DAC
  units, 0.5~s per point.
\item Hold-delay scans: trigger limited to one channel (the shooting channel); spectrum of
  every channel at each delay, 10\,000 events. 101, 104--106: 25~ns, channels 0, 20, 40, 60,
  0--248~ns; 102, 103: 50~ns, channel 0, 0--496~ns.
\item The DAQ applies a new delay with a lag of up to five points; those points are left out.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Menu 2: method}
\begin{itemize}
\item Staircase: below the edge $\theta^\mathrm{e}$ the discriminator fires on the noise of the
  fast shaper (plateau); above it the rate falls in steps, one per photoelectron, $\Delta\theta$
  apart.
\item Hold-delay scan: the spectrum of the shooting channel is a ladder of 1, 2, 3~pe. If the
  hold comes before the maximum of the pulse, the 1-pe peak sits below the full amplitude;
  once the window contains the maximum, it stays there. The 1-pe peak against the delay is
  the response of the peak detector; the mean also counts the dark pulses in the window.
\end{itemize}
\begin{align*}
q_1(\tau) &= \text{position of the 1-pe peak at delay } \tau, \qquad
G = \operatorname{median}_{\text{last 8 points}} q_1(\tau) - p_i, \\
\tau_\mathrm{min} &= \min\{\tau : q_1(\tau) - p_i \ge 0.95\,G\}.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item $G$ gain at this bias; $\tau_\mathrm{min}$ minimum hold delay; model of the front end:
  maximum of the shaped pulse 31~ns (25~ns) and 58~ns (50~ns) after the threshold crossing.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Menu 2: summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item A threshold of 185 DAC units is a clean single-photoelectron trigger: the noise edge is
  at 179--182 and one photoelectron 20 units.
\item The response reaches its plateau at 32--40~ns for 25~ns of shaping (model 31~ns) and at
  128--160~ns for 50~ns (model 58~ns); the stored amplitude then stays constant, as for an
  ideal peak detector.
\item Minimum hold delay: 40~ns at 25~ns, 160~ns at 50~ns.
\item One photoelectron: 20 units of the threshold DAC (fast shaper); 150--182 counts of the
  high-gain ADC (slow shaper, 25~ns).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Menu 3: single-photoelectron calibration}

\begin{frame}{Menu 3: dishes served, data}
\begin{columns}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Dishes served}
\begin{itemize}
\item The gain, in ADC counts per photoelectron, is the unit of the whole chain.
\item With a periodic trigger the window holds the pedestal or the dark pulse that fell
  inside: the spectrum is a ladder of 0, 1, 2, \ldots~pe; the spacing is the gain.
\item For every channel: the gain at three biases and three shapings; the breakdown voltage;
  the pedestal noise of menu~1 in photoelectrons.
\end{itemize}
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{Data}, boards 1, 2, 4, 50\,000 events per board, HoldDelay 200~ns
\begin{itemize}
\item Runs 108--110: 43~V at 25, 50, 87.5~ns.
\item Runs 111, 112: 42 and 41~V at 25~ns.
\item Staircases 113 (43~V), 114, 115 (41~V), board 1.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Menu 3: method}
\begin{align*}
f(x) &= \sum_{n=0}^{5} A_n \exp\left[-\frac{(x - p - n g)^2}{2(\sigma_0^2 + n\sigma_1^2)}\right],
\qquad \text{resolved if } \sqrt{\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2} < g/2, \\
g &= a\,(V - V_\mathrm{bd}), \qquad \sigma^\mathrm{pe} = \sigma / g.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item A comb of six Gaussians per channel: $p$ pedestal, $g$ gain, $\sigma_0$ width of the
  0-pe peak, $\sigma_1$ extra width per photoelectron; resolved when the 1-pe peak is narrower
  than half the spacing.
\item Seed: the peaks at 41~V, then scaled by the overvoltage.
\item Breakdown $V_\mathrm{bd}$: the line through the gain at 41, 42, 43~V.
\item Noise in photoelectrons: the pedestal noise of menu~1 over the median gain of the board.
\item Blind channels left out.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Menu 3: summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item The single-photoelectron calibration works at 25~ns: ladder resolved in nearly every
  channel at the three biases; gain 165--178 counts per pe at 43~V, uniform to 2.4--5.6\%.
\item Breakdown 38.8--39.0~V on the three boards; 43~V is 4.1~V above breakdown.
\item The pedestal noise of the chain is 0.07 photoelectrons per channel, half coherent; as
  the model at 87.5~ns, half the model at 25~ns. For the energy at 87.5~ns, 64 channels:
  3.6~pe coherent, 0.4 incoherent, against 2500~pe of a 511~keV event in BGO.
\item Open: entries at 0 growing with the shaping (F12).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Towards menu 4: calibration at 87.5 ns}
\begin{itemize}
\item The crystal data are taken at 87.5~ns, in both gains.
\item High gain: $g_i(87.5) = r_b\, g_i(25)$, with $r_b$ the ratio of the gains at 87.5 and
  25~ns on the channels resolved at both: 1.006 (26 channels, rms 0.044); per board 1.011,
  0.981, 1.012.
\item Low gain: single photoelectrons not resolvable (17 counts per pe against 6 of noise).
  Menu 4, on the first crystal runs: per channel, a line of $\mathrm{LG} - p_\mathrm{LG}$
  against $\mathrm{HG} - p_\mathrm{HG}$ (high gain below its ceiling, low gain above its
  noise) through the origin; its inverse slope $r_i$ gives $g^\mathrm{LG}_i = g_i(87.5)/r_i$.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
